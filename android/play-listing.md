## Full description (4,000 characters max)

```
Resus Assist helps a trained team keep time and keep a record during a resuscitation attempt. It follows the published Resuscitation Council UK 2025 algorithms for adults (ALS), children (PLS) and newborns (NLS).

During the attempt:
• Arrest timer and a 2-minute cycle timer, with a spoken and visual countdown to each rhythm and pulse check (a 5-second countdown to each heart-rate check for newborns).
• A CPR metronome, and sound that can be turned off while the flashing countdown stays.
• Rhythm buttons (shockable and non-shockable) that lead you through the next step, with shock energies for the protocol.
• Tap to record shocks, adrenaline, amiodarone and other interventions; each is time-stamped, and any entry made in error can be undone.
• The reversible causes and special circumstances, to work through as a team.
• Start the clock when the patient arrives already in arrest, and enter cycles, shocks and drugs given before arrival.
• For children, an optional WETFLAG calculator from age or weight. For newborns, inflation breaths, ventilation and chest compressions steps.
• Post-resuscitation care prompts once circulation returns.

Afterwards:
• A time-stamped record of the attempt to copy into the notes or print, with a handover notes box.

Works with no signal: everything is inside the app. The current record is kept only on this phone, is cleared by New Patient, and expires after 12 hours. The screen stays on while the app is open, and the back button asks before closing.

Important:
• Resus Assist is an aid for trained healthcare staff. It is not a medical device. It does not replace training, local protocols or clinical judgement, and you remain responsible for every decision and dose.
• Do not enter patient names or identifiers; the app does not ask for any.
• It is independent: Resuscitation Council UK has not produced or endorsed it. Always check against the current guidelines and your hospital's policies.

No accounts, no adverts and no tracking. The app collects no personal data.

Published by West Midlands Evidence Based Emergency Medicine Ltd (EM Evidence).
```

Points checked against the code on 29 October 2026: the three protocols, arrest and cycle timers, spoken

post-resuscitation care, copy and print of the record, handover notes, 12-hour expiry.
